If you're spending ₹50,000 to ₹1 lakh a month on Meta ads in India, you're above the platform's survival threshold but below what most campaigns need to actually learn. That gap is where most small business budgets get wasted — not on bad creative, but on being split across too many ad sets to ever gather enough signal.

The fix isn't more budget. It's less fragmentation.

Meta will technically let you run an ad set on very little. [Meta's own minimum is $1 a day](https://en-gb.facebook.com/business/help/203183363050448) for most impression-charged campaigns. But a technical minimum and a working minimum are different things, and at ₹50k–₹1L a month, the honest answer is: pick one offer, put it in one or two ad sets, and don't touch it until you have real data.

## Why ₹50k–₹1 lakh a month feels like enough and isn't

₹50,000 to ₹1,00,000 a month works out well above both thresholds that matter. It clears [Meta's $1/day minimum](https://en-gb.facebook.com/business/help/203183363050448) comfortably, and it clears the ₹300–₹500/day working floor that [Inflex Media](https://inflexmedia.com/blogs/meta-ads-budget-in-india/) describes as the point where the algorithm starts finding the right audience.

The problem shows up when that monthly budget gets split. Run three or four ad sets at once — one for cold audiences, one for retargeting, one or two "testing" new creative — and each individual ad set falls back below the ₹300–₹500/day floor, even though the total monthly figure looks healthy on paper. The budget hasn't shrunk. The signal reaching each ad set has.

Meta's own guidance on cost-cap bidding makes the same point from a different angle: [a cost cap needs a daily budget at least five times the cap itself](https://en-gb.facebook.com/business/help/203183363050448), because the algorithm needs room to find enough conversions to optimise toward. Split the budget and you take that room away from every ad set at once.

What to do about it: before you add a second ad set, ask whether the first one has 50 conversions yet. If it doesn't, you don't have a testing budget. You have one ad set's worth of money, however many campaigns it's divided into.

## The one opinion a cautious agency won't give you

A lot of agency advice at this budget level is "test multiple creatives and audiences to see what works." That's correct advice for a much larger monthly budget. At ₹50k–₹1L, it's a slower way to lose the money.

My read: testing is a luxury you fund with volume, and you don't have volume yet at this spend. [Lead-gen campaigns in the ₹15,000–₹40,000/month range produce 30–200 leads a month](https://www.vgraple.com/blog/facebook-ads-cost-india-2026/) according to Vgraple's benchmarks — split that kind of budget across four ad sets and each one sees a fraction of those leads. No algorithm learns from a handful of conversions spread across unrelated ad sets.

The agency that tells you to diversify at this budget isn't wrong about diversification being good eventually. It's wrong about the order of operations. Concentrate first, diversify once concentration has given you enough data to know what's actually working.

## What one ad set, properly funded, should look like

Pick one offer — one product, one form, one WhatsApp number to message. Put the full daily budget behind it in one or, at most, two ad sets: one cold, one retargeting the people who engaged but didn't convert.

[Advantage+ campaigns consolidate targeting decisions](https://celorisdesigns.com/blog/meta-advantage-plus-vs-manual-2026-guide) into fewer discrete ad sets than manual campaign structures, which is exactly the direction you want to move in at this budget — fewer decision points for Meta to split signal across, not more.

Let it run. [₹80–₹350 per qualified lead](https://celorisdesigns.com/blog/meta-advantage-plus-vs-manual-2026-guide) is the India-wide range, and at this monthly spend, that range puts you in the same territory as the 30–200 leads/month seen at the ₹15,000–₹40,000 budget tier, scaled up with your larger budget. Give it at least a week, ideally two, before judging it. Pulling an ad set a few days in because the cost per lead looks high is the second most common way this budget gets wasted.

## What changes once leads actually start arriving

The volume problem doesn't stop at the ad account. A lead that sits in a spreadsheet for hours is a lead you paid ₹80–₹350 for and then threw away. At low volume this is forgivable because you can call people back yourself. As volume climbs toward the upper end of the Vgraple range, it stops being forgivable, because no founder is calling back dozens of people same-day while also running the business.

This is the part people skip: the ad budget and the follow-up system have to scale together. A WhatsApp auto-reply or a simple CRM trigger that fires the moment a form submits costs nothing to set up and changes the conversion rate on every rupee you've already spent.

## What I'd do Monday

1. **Count your current ad sets.** If you have more than two running at once on a sub-₹1L monthly budget, pause all but the best performer by current cost per lead.
2. **Check each ad set against the floor.** Work out roughly what each ad set gets per day from your monthly budget. If any one is below the ₹300–₹500/day working floor, consolidate.
3. **Pick one offer and commit for two weeks.** No swapping creative, no adding audiences, until you have at least 50 conversions on the current one.
4. **Set up the follow-up before you scale spend.** A same-day WhatsApp or call-back trigger on every form submission matters more as lead volume rises than a bigger budget does.
5. **Review cost per lead against the ₹80–₹350 India range**, not against what you hoped it would be — then decide whether to add the second ad set.
