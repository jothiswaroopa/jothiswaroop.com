If you manufacture industrial equipment and you're deciding between Google Search and Performance Max for B2B lead generation, run Search first. Not alongside PMax. Not as a 20% test budget sitting next to it. Instead of it, for 30 days.

Here's why this matters this week and not next quarter. Google's own best-practice guide tells advertisers to give Performance Max "at least 6 weeks" before judging or adjusting it, so its automation can learn. If you're running your own ad account and you need to know whether Google Ads actually produces buyers for a six-figure machine, you don't have six weeks of patience before the first real signal. You have a month, maybe less, before the budget conversation happens again.

The answer, in one line: Search campaigns let you see and control exactly which buyer-intent phrases bring in enquiries, while Performance Max is built to spend across Search, YouTube, Display, Discover, Gmail and Maps based on vague "audience signals" you can't fully inspect. For a founder selling a narrow, high-ticket product to a small buyer pool, that scale works against you.

## What Performance Max is actually built for

Performance Max is a scale product. Google's own help page says it's designed to find leads "across Search, YouTube, Display, Discover, Gmail, and Maps" using machine-learning signals you feed it, not keywords you choose. That's the whole pitch: hand over a few audience signals, a budget and some creative, and let the system find demand wherever it lives.

By April 2025, Google said PMax was used by "over one million advertisers," according to [Google's official blog](https://blog.google/products/ads-commerce/channel-performance-reporting-coming-to-performance-max/). That scale is the problem for a manufacturer, not the solution. A product built to serve a million advertisers across six channels is tuned for breadth. Your buyer for a CNC machine or an industrial lifting system is not broad. It's a plant manager or a procurement lead typing a very specific phrase into Search, not browsing YouTube or checking Gmail promotions.

What changes for you: if your buyer pool is narrow and your sales cycle is long, PMax's strength — finding more people, more places — becomes a weakness. It spends your budget finding adjacent, semantically similar traffic that isn't your buyer, because that's what the system is designed to do. Search, by contrast, only shows your ad when someone types a phrase you've chosen to bid on, and you see every search term that triggered it.

## Why scale works against a narrow buyer list

The clearest evidence is a documented case, not a hypothetical. An industrial handling-equipment advertiser running Smart/PMax campaigns found the system counting delivery-driver Maps navigation clicks as "leads," and matching the search term "lifting aid" to elderly-care and mobility-equipment searches rather than warehouse handling equipment, according to [Dirk Röttges's case study](https://www.dirkroettges.de/google-ads-b2b-case-study-industrial-handling-technology/). The advertiser reverted to standard Search campaigns afterwards.

This isn't a one-off glitch. It's the predictable outcome of an automated system matching on broad semantic signals across six channels, rather than exact buyer-intent phrases. "Lifting aid" means two completely different things to a warehouse manager and to a home-care buyer. A human running a Search campaign would exclude the second group with a negative keyword in five minutes. PMax, working from signals rather than explicit exclusions, doesn't know to.

What to do about it: before you let an automated system decide where your budget goes, you need a channel that shows you the actual search term behind every click, so you can see this kind of mismatch happening and fix it immediately rather than six weeks later.

## The black box Google only opened in 2025

For years, advertisers couldn't see which individual channel, or which search term, inside a Performance Max campaign was actually producing results. That changed on 30 April 2025, when Google [rolled out full search-term reporting inside PMax](https://blog.google/products/ads-commerce/channel-performance-reporting-coming-to-performance-max/) — a feature Search campaigns have had since they existed. Google also said it shipped "more than 90 quality improvements" to PMax in 2024 alone, lifting conversions and conversion value "by more than 10%."

Read that as what it is: an admission that the product needed fixing, repeatedly, for years, before advertisers could even see what it was doing with their money. If you're testing channel fit for the first time this month, you're testing a product that Google itself was still actively patching twelve months ago.

What changes for you: you no longer have to accept "trust the algorithm" as an answer, because full reporting exists now. But visibility into a bad decision isn't the same as a good decision. The reporting is new. The underlying scale-first design hasn't changed.

## What "qualified lead" actually means

Google Ads Help is explicit that a default lead-form conversion counts any submitted form as a conversion, full stop. To get anything better, you have to set up a ["Qualified Lead" or "Converted Lead" goal](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/13775965?hl=en), which Google says gives you "enhanced protections against invalid lead activity." Read between the lines: the basic version doesn't protect you from invalid leads at all.

For a manufacturer, this is the step most founders get wrong, on Search or PMax. They look at "47 leads this month" and treat it as a result, when half of it is students researching a college project, competitors checking pricing, or someone who filled the form by mistake. The fix isn't a Google Ads setting. It's deciding, before you launch anything, what a real lead looks like for your business — a phone number that answers, a company that exists, a budget that's plausible — and only counting that number when you judge the campaign.

Google's own [lead-gen guide](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/15795510) frames Search as reaching "business-relevant, valuable searches" through broad match on chosen terms. That framing only holds if you've actually built the keyword list around how your buyer searches, not around what you sell. A buyer doesn't search "heavy industrial lifting solutions provider." They search what their problem is: "warehouse pallet lifter 2 tonne," "forklift alternative narrow aisle." Build the list from their language, not your brochure.

## What I'd do Monday

1. **Pause or don't launch Performance Max.** If it's already running, switch it off rather than letting it run alongside Search for the first month — you want a clean signal, not a blended one you can't separate.
2. **Build one Search campaign with 15–25 exact and phrase-match keywords**, written the way a buyer would type the problem, not the way your catalogue describes the product. Add negative keywords for consumer, hobbyist and navigational terms on day one.
3. **Set bidding to Maximize Clicks or manual CPC**, not a Smart Bidding strategy that needs weeks of conversion data to calibrate. You're testing demand, not optimising yet.
4. **Define a qualified lead before you launch**, in writing: a working phone number, a real company domain, a stated budget range or project timeline. Track only that number against spend, every week.
5. **Review search terms weekly, not monthly.** Exclude anything that isn't your buyer the moment you see it, rather than waiting for a scheduled optimisation.
6. **At day 30, write down cost per qualified lead and decide from that number**, not from total form-fills, whether Search alone justifies more budget before you ever test Performance Max again.
